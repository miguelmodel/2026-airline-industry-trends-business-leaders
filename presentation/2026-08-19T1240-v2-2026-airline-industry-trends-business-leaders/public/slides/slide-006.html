Hedge figures approximate; forecasts assume supply eases into 2027

The point for leaders is conditionality: these forecasts assume the supply disruption eases and production recovers in early 2027, so the 2026-2027 path is unusually sensitive to whether that holds.

Explain the crack-spread nuance plainly: because most hedges are on crude rather than jet fuel, the record refining margin sits largely uncovered even for carriers that hedge heavily.

Note the regional dispersion in hedging as neutral evidence — European carriers entered 2026 far more hedged than North American majors, which changes near-term cost exposure by region.

The hedge-coverage figures are approximate and secondary: the global roughly one-third, Europe near 70%, and minimal-to-zero for North American majors come from a secondary summary of the IATA Global Outlook (The Frequent Flier, Jun 2026), with carrier-level positions compiled from company disclosures (Reuters via RTE, 3 Mar 2026); the crude-versus-jet hedge-basis point is from the IATA Fuel Fact Sheet.

Transition: fuel is the acute 2026 cost; the slower-moving constraints are on the supply of aircraft, engines, and people.
