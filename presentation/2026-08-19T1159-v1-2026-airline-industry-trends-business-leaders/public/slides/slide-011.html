Digital and Commercial

Commercial Engines Rest on a Trust Foundation

Ancillary revenue $157B estimated in 2025 — equal to 15.7% of total airline revenue.
Loyalty & co-brand ~$8B Delta's American Express relationship — about 10% of 2025 revenue.
Payment mix 72% · 28% Card use down from 79% to 72%, digital wallets up to 28%, and half of travelers now use biometrics.
Trust & security — the counterweight ~46% of online payment fraud has historically hit airlines; 2025 saw ten major cyber incidents, incl. a breach exposing 5.7M customers.

Present these as four connected commercial pillars: ancillaries and co-brand loyalty are large, relatively stable profit engines distinct from ticket yield.

The payment-mix and biometric data show passengers moving to digital and self-service, but adoption is tied to trust, which is why the fraud and cyber figures belong on the same slide.

Note the nuance that measured fraud rates actually fell about 30% to 0.25% of transactions even as airlines remained the most-targeted sector, and that the largest 2025 exposures came through third parties; biometric users reported 85% satisfaction.

Provenance and caveats: the ~46% online-payment-fraud share, the >$1 billion in annual losses, and the ~30% decline to 0.25% are historical figures from a secondary trade report citing IATA and Accertify (Open Jaw, 2 Sep 2025); the count of ten major 2025 cyber incidents is a secondary compilation citing SOCRadar (Aerospace Global News, 2025), while the 5.7 million-customer exposure is from Qantas's own disclosure.

Transition: the same year brought a step-up in regulatory and sustainability obligations.
