This is the most disruptive fleet-availability constraint: durability issues on new single-aisle engines pulled aircraft out of service faster than shops could return them.

For operators, the forward risk is the maintenance supercycle — shop-visit demand multiplies through the 2030s and 2040s while the fleet ages, so maintenance timelines increasingly shape schedules and unit costs.

Keep the MRO and fleet-age figures labeled as provider forecasts and scope-dependent estimates; the ~13-year figure is the broad fleet average, with the passenger-only figure near 12.8 years.

The easing trajectory and timeline are program-specific and reported by trade press: manufacturers report A220 and E2 GTF issues largely resolved by end-2026, while an A320neo (PW1100G) operator quoted in AIN/Leeham expects two to three more years, so treat the recovery timeline as a manufacturer-and-operator statement rather than a settled forecast.

Transition: even with aircraft and engines available, reliability depends on people and airspace capacity.
