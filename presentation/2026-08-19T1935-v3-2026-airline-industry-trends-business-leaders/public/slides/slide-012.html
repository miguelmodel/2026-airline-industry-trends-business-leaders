Regulation and Resilience

Carbon Costs Rise Faster Than SAF Supply

EU SAF mandate (ReFuelEU): 2% → 70% Delivered SAF supply (~0.8%)
EU ETS free allowances end 2026 · EUA ~€70–95/t SAF ~2.4 Mt in 2026 · ~$4.3B added cost e-SAF ~0.02 Mt vs ~0.6 Mt mandated by 2030 UK 2% → 10% (2030) → 22% (2040) · CORSIA 2027

The throughline for this section is a widening gap between binding obligations and delivered capacity, most visible in carbon costs and SAF.

For finance and sustainability leaders, note that the EU ETS free-allowance phase-out turns carbon into a full cash cost from 2026, and CORSIA shifts from its voluntary first phase to mandatory participation in 2027 (per EASA), so emissions costs are now recurring line items that interact with fuel economics.

The 70 to 95 euros per tonne figure is a traded market range from secondary carbon-market reporting (euenergy.live, Apr 2026), not a fixed or official price, and should be presented as a market estimate.

Define the term for non-specialists: e-SAF (electro-SAF, or power-to-liquid) is synthetic jet fuel made from green hydrogen and captured carbon dioxide, the scarcest and most expensive SAF pathway.

Keep SAF figures labeled as estimates; the e-SAF shortfall is the binding bottleneck, with very little announced capacity having reached final investment decision.

Transition: alongside carbon, consumer rules, insurance, and climate exposure round out the resilience picture.
