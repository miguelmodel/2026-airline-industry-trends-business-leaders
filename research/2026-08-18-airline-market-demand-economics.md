## 1. Executive Summary

The central 2026 story in airline market economics is a wide gap between an optimistic pre-year forecast and a sharply weaker mid-year revision, set against demand that started strong and then contracted. In its December 2025 outlook, the International Air Transport Association (IATA) projected a record industry **net profit of $41 billion in 2026** at a **3.9% net margin**, on revenue of **$1.053 trillion**, **5.2 billion passengers**, and a record **83.8% load factor** ([IATA, 9 Dec 2025](https://www.iata.org/en/pressroom/2025-releases/2025-12-09-01/)). By June 2026, IATA had **roughly halved** the profit projection to **$23.0 billion** at a **2.0% net margin**, driven principally by a sharp rise in jet-fuel and other input costs ([IATA Global Outlook, Jun 2026](https://www.iata.org/en/publications/economics/reports/global-outlook-for-air-transport-june-2026/)).

Realized 2026 demand data tracked this deterioration. Global revenue passenger kilometers (RPK) rose **3.8% year-on-year in January 2026** ([IATA, 2 Mar 2026](https://www.iata.org/en/pressroom/2026-releases/2026-03-02-02/)), then turned negative by mid-year, falling **2.2% in May and 1.7% in June 2026** — the first year-on-year decline in more than two years — even as load factors held at record levels near **84%** ([IATA APMA June 2026](https://www.iata.org/en/iata-repository/publications/economic-reports/air-passenger-market-analysis-june-2026/); [IATA, 30 Jul 2026](https://www.iata.org/en/pressroom/2026-releases/07-30-air-passenger-demand-falls-june/)). The decline was concentrated rather than broad: excluding one region, June global demand fell only **0.6%** and international demand excluding that region still rose, indicating most markets remained resilient ([IATA, 30 Jul 2026](https://www.iata.org/en/pressroom/2026-releases/07-30-air-passenger-demand-falls-june/)).

Underneath the aggregate, dispersion is the defining feature. Revenue growth stayed robust while margins diverged sharply by region and by business model: European full-service carriers narrowed first-quarter losses while European low-cost carriers deepened theirs, and North American carriers posted record quarterly revenue on unit-revenue strength rather than volume ([IBA, 22 May 2026](https://www.iba.aero/resources/articles/2026-q1-airline-results/)). Demand is increasingly **yield-led rather than volume-led**: business-travel spending is forecast to reach a record **$1.71 trillion in 2026 (+7.2%)** while trip counts rise only **1.3%**, reflecting higher spend per trip ([CAPA / GBTA, Aug 2026](https://www.routesonline.com/suppliers/10554/capa-centre-for-aviation/news/299666765/business-travel-hits-record-spending-but-airlines-face-a-harder-growth-equation/)). Longer term, Airbus projects passenger traffic growing **3.6% annually** through 2044 toward more than 10 billion passengers, keeping structural demand intact beyond the 2026 cost surge ([Airbus GMF 2025](https://www.airbus.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-airbus-global-market-forecast-2025-people-and-commerce-driving-air-traffic-growth)).

The through-line for the presentation: 2026 combines **structurally strong demand and record load factors** with **thin, cost-sensitive margins that remain below the cost of capital**, so profitability is highly exposed to input-cost swings and shows large regional and business-model dispersion.

## 2. Evidence Window and Methodology

- **Evidence window:** Sources published from December 2024 through 31 July 2026, emphasizing 2025-2026 material. All web pages accessed on **2026-08-18**.
- **Geographic scope:** Global scheduled passenger airlines (network, low-cost, and regional). Cargo is included only where it bears on passenger-airline revenue mix or belly economics.
- **Source strategy:** Primary industry and official evidence was prioritized — IATA financial outlooks and monthly air-passenger market analyses (the recognized industry aggregate), and a manufacturer long-term forecast (Airbus). Analyst and association data (IBA market intelligence; GBTA business-travel data reported via CAPA) supplement carrier-model and demand-mix detail and are labeled as such. Two secondary trade-press reports corroborate the June 2026 realized data.
- **Labeling convention:** Each figure is tagged **[Realized]** (reported historical outcome), **[Estimate]** (current-year estimate that may still be revised), or **[Forecast]** with vintage (for example, *Forecast — Dec 2025* vs *Forecast — Jun 2026*). Partial-year or forecast values are never presented as realized full-year results.
- **Metric definitions used by sources:** RPK = revenue passenger kilometers (demand); ASK = available seat kilometers (capacity); PLF = passenger load factor (RPK/ASK); yield = revenue per RPK; ATK = available tonne kilometers; ROIC = return on invested capital; WACC = weighted average cost of capital. Currencies are US dollars unless stated. IATA figures are industry aggregates; carrier figures are individual-airline disclosures.
- **Neutrality handling:** Several 2026 sources attribute weaker outcomes to specific events. This report restates only the neutral cost, operating, and market evidence and omits event narratives, consistent with the binding brief.
- **Preflight validation:** A `WebIQ-MCP/web` query returned usable IATA primary sources, and a `WebIQ-MCP/browse` call on the IATA December 2025 release returned full, meaningful content; both are retained in this report. A second `browse` call (IBA) validated successfully. During the round-2 revision, an additional `web`+`browse` pass validated the neutral IATA Global Outlook (June 2026) economic report now used for the mid-year figures.

## 3. Key Findings (Dated)

1. **[Forecast — Dec 2025] Record profit projected for 2026 at a thin margin.** IATA projected 2026 net profit of **$41 billion** (net margin **3.9%**, unchanged from 2025), operating profit **$72.8 billion** (6.9% operating margin), and **ROIC 6.8%** — still below an estimated **8.2% WACC** ([IATA, 9 Dec 2025](https://www.iata.org/en/pressroom/2025-releases/2025-12-09-01/)).
2. **[Forecast — Jun 2026] Mid-year projection halved.** By June 2026, IATA revised 2026 net profit to **$23.0 billion** (net margin **2.0%**), with ROIC **4.3%** against a higher **8.5% WACC** — attributed principally to a sharp jet-fuel price surge and higher input costs ([IATA Global Outlook, Jun 2026](https://www.iata.org/en/publications/economics/reports/global-outlook-for-air-transport-june-2026/)).
3. **[Realized] Demand started strong then contracted.** RPK grew **3.8% in January 2026** ([IATA, 2 Mar 2026](https://www.iata.org/en/pressroom/2026-releases/2026-03-02-02/)) but fell **2.2% in May** and **1.7% in June** year-on-year ([IATA APMA June 2026](https://www.iata.org/en/iata-repository/publications/economic-reports/air-passenger-market-analysis-june-2026/)).
4. **[Realized] Weakness was concentrated, not broad.** Excluding one region, June global demand fell only **0.6%**, and international demand excluding that region **rose 1.1%** ([IATA, 30 Jul 2026](https://www.iata.org/en/pressroom/2026-releases/07-30-air-passenger-demand-falls-june/); corroborated by [AirGuide, 31 Jul 2026](https://airguide.info/iata-global-air-passenger-demand-falls-1-7-in-june-2026/) and [Travel Trade Journal, 31 Jul 2026](https://traveltradejournal.com/global-air-passenger-demand-falls-1-7-per-cent-in-june-as-domestic-markets-weigh-on-growth-iata/)).
5. **[Realized/Forecast] Load factors set records despite the demand dip.** Load factor was a January record **82.0%**, a June record-tier **84.2%**, and is forecast near **84.0%** for full-year 2026 as capacity stays constrained ([IATA, 2 Mar 2026](https://www.iata.org/en/pressroom/2026-releases/2026-03-02-02/); [IATA Global Outlook, Jun 2026](https://www.iata.org/en/publications/economics/reports/global-outlook-for-air-transport-june-2026/)).
6. **[Forecast] Revenue rose even as profit fell.** The June revision raised the 2026 revenue forecast to **$1.165 trillion (+9.4%)** — above the December **$1.053 trillion** — because fares rose to recoup costs, while net profit still halved ([IATA Global Outlook, Jun 2026](https://www.iata.org/en/publications/economics/reports/global-outlook-for-air-transport-june-2026/)).
7. **[Forecast] Fares and yields turned from flat to rising.** December 2025 assumed roughly flat yields; the June revision assumed passenger yields **+7%** as carriers passed through higher input costs ([IATA, 9 Dec 2025](https://www.iata.org/en/pressroom/2025-releases/2025-12-09-01/); [IATA Global Outlook, Jun 2026](https://www.iata.org/en/publications/economics/reports/global-outlook-for-air-transport-june-2026/)).
8. **[Realized/Forecast] Margin dispersion widened by model.** In Q1 2026, European full-service carriers improved average EBIT margin from **-4.0% to -0.7%** while European low-cost carriers deepened losses despite **+21% revenue** ([IBA, 22 May 2026](https://www.iba.aero/resources/articles/2026-q1-airline-results/)).
9. **[Forecast] Business travel is value-dense, not volume-led.** 2026 business-travel spend is forecast at **$1.71 trillion (+7.2%)** on only **+1.3%** more trips (~1.84 billion) ([CAPA / GBTA, Aug 2026](https://www.routesonline.com/suppliers/10554/capa-centre-for-aviation/news/299666765/business-travel-hits-record-spending-but-airlines-face-a-harder-growth-equation/)).
10. **[Forecast] Structural long-run growth remains intact.** Airbus projects **3.6% annual** passenger-traffic growth through 2044 ([Airbus GMF 2025](https://www.airbus.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-airbus-global-market-forecast-2025-people-and-commerce-driving-air-traffic-growth)).

## 5. Regional Variation

**December 2025 baseline.** IATA projected Europe as the strongest region in absolute profit (**$14.0 B**, 4.9% margin) and the Middle East as the strongest by margin and profit per passenger (**9.3%**, **$28.60/pax**). North America profit was stable but had ceded the top-profit ranking to Europe. Asia Pacific carried the fastest capacity and demand growth (RPK +7.3%) but the thinnest margins among large regions (2.3%), with load factors projected to a regional record **84.4%**. Africa remained structurally thin-margin, with the highest unit costs globally (cost per ATK near **140 US cents**, roughly double the industry average) ([IATA, 9 Dec 2025](https://www.iata.org/en/pressroom/2025-releases/2025-12-09-01/)).

**June 2026 revision.** The mid-year update lowered projected profitability across all regions, with the industry still expected to remain profitable in aggregate for the year ([IATA Global Outlook, Jun 2026](https://www.iata.org/en/publications/economics/reports/global-outlook-for-air-transport-june-2026/)).

**Realized June 2026 divergence.** The monthly data confirmed the divergence: Europe led major regions on international demand (**+1.5%**, highest load factor at **87.1%**), Latin America and Africa grew fastest (**+3.5%** and **+6.7%** international), Asia Pacific was roughly flat internationally (**+0.4%**) with short-haul capacity trimmed amid higher fuel costs, North America dipped (**-1.0%** international), and the Middle East saw international traffic fall about **14%**. Domestic markets were the main drag globally (domestic RPK **-3.0%**), with China (**-5.2%**) and Japan (**-3.8%**) the weakest large domestic markets and Brazil the only major domestic market to grow ([IATA, 30 Jul 2026](https://www.iata.org/en/pressroom/2026-releases/07-30-air-passenger-demand-falls-june/) and [IATA APMA June 2026](https://www.iata.org/en/iata-repository/publications/economic-reports/air-passenger-market-analysis-june-2026/); corroborated by [AirGuide, 31 Jul 2026](https://airguide.info/iata-global-air-passenger-demand-falls-1-7-in-june-2026/) and [Travel Trade Journal, 31 Jul 2026](https://traveltradejournal.com/global-air-passenger-demand-falls-1-7-per-cent-in-june-as-domestic-markets-weigh-on-growth-iata/)).

On IATA's figures, excluding one region, June global demand declined only **0.6%** and international demand excluding that region rose **1.1%** year-on-year, indicating the aggregate dip was concentrated rather than broad ([IATA, 30 Jul 2026](https://www.iata.org/en/pressroom/2026-releases/07-30-air-passenger-demand-falls-june/)).

The neutral reading is that the mid-year softening was concentrated — in one region and in several large domestic markets where higher fuel prices fed into fares — while the majority of international markets kept growing ([IATA, 30 Jul 2026](https://www.iata.org/en/pressroom/2026-releases/07-30-air-passenger-demand-falls-june/)).

## 6. Carrier-Model Variation and Margin Dispersion

Business-model dispersion widened in 2026. IATA's December baseline already flagged divergent trajectories: in Europe, low-cost carriers were "expanding at double-digit rates and outperforming full-service carriers on net profit margin," while in North America low-cost carriers were "under pressure," exposed to a shrinking domestic segment, a shift in passenger preference toward premium products, and single-fleet-type disadvantages amid supply-chain constraints ([IATA, 9 Dec 2025](https://www.iata.org/en/pressroom/2025-releases/2025-12-09-01/)).

First-quarter 2026 airline disclosures, compiled by IBA, show revenue resilience alongside uneven earnings quality ([IBA, 22 May 2026](https://www.iba.aero/resources/articles/2026-q1-airline-results/)):

- **North America:** aggregate revenue up ~**10%** while ASKs rose only ~**2%**, i.e., unit-revenue-led growth. American reported record Q1 revenue of **$13.9 B (+10.8%)**; Delta guided to low-teens Q2 revenue growth on flat capacity while flagging higher fuel to recover; Air Canada posted record Q1 operating revenue of **C$5.8 B** but suspended full-year 2026 guidance citing second-half fuel-price volatility.
- **Europe:** strongest top-line growth (~**+18%** revenue), but mixed profitability. Full-service carriers improved average EBIT margin from **-4.0% to -0.7%**; low-cost carriers deepened losses despite **+21%** revenue (Q1 is seasonally weak for leisure-heavy LCCs).
- **Asia Pacific:** among early reporters, EBIT margin expanded to **5.6%** on **+6%** ASKs, though IBA cautions the sample was limited.

> "European full-service carriers materially reduced losses, improving EBIT margin from an average of -4.0% to -0.7%, while European low-cost carriers saw losses deepen despite 21% revenue growth."
> - Source: [IBA, 2026 Q1 Airline Results (22 May 2026)](https://www.iba.aero/resources/articles/2026-q1-airline-results/)

IBA also characterized the quarter as "an early stress test of pricing power, fuel hedge performance and network flexibility," noting that carriers' hedge positions differed materially (for example, one European network carrier reported an 80% 2026 fuel hedge ratio, delaying the visible cost impact) ([IBA, 22 May 2026](https://www.iba.aero/resources/articles/2026-q1-airline-results/)). Separately, IBA flagged stress among lease-heavy ultra-low-cost carriers, citing the collapse of Spirit Airlines as a marker of that segment's fragility — a neutral market signal of how thin-margin, single-model carriers absorb cost shocks differently than diversified network carriers.

## 7. Demand Mix and Traveler Behavior

**Business travel: spend-led, not trip-led.** GBTA's Business Travel Index, reported via CAPA, forecasts global business-travel spending at a record **$1.71 trillion in 2026 (+7.2%)** while the number of business trips rises only **1.3%** to about **1.84 billion**. That implies spend per trip rising from roughly **$870 (2025)** to about **$930 (2026)**. The 2025 result was already spend-led: expenditure grew **8.4%** to **$1.59 trillion**, ahead of the earlier 6.6% forecast ([CAPA / GBTA, Aug 2026](https://www.routesonline.com/suppliers/10554/capa-centre-for-aviation/news/299666765/business-travel-hits-record-spending-but-airlines-face-a-harder-growth-equation/)).

> "Global business travel spending is forecast to reach USD1.71 trillion in 2026 (+7.2%) while trips rise only 1.3% to ~1.84 billion, indicating higher spend per trip rather than major volume growth."
> - Source: [CAPA Centre for Aviation, reporting GBTA (Aug 2026)](https://www.routesonline.com/suppliers/10554/capa-centre-for-aviation/news/299666765/business-travel-hits-record-spending-but-airlines-face-a-harder-growth-equation/)

**Premium demand and concentration.** CAPA/GBTA report that about **42%** of business travelers typically fly in premium cabins and that business-travel value is highly concentrated: the top 15 markets represent about **84%** of 2026 spend, and two markets (United States and China) together account for roughly **48%**. Asia Pacific is the largest business-travel region (over **$700 billion** forecast in 2026), though rail competition is significant there (about **72%** of Asia Pacific business travelers use rail). The Americas are supported by technology and AI investment cycles, with Brazil business spend forecast up **13.8%** ([CAPA / GBTA, Aug 2026](https://www.routesonline.com/suppliers/10554/capa-centre-for-aviation/news/299666765/business-travel-hits-record-spending-but-airlines-face-a-harder-growth-equation/)). IATA's December baseline separately noted a passenger-preference shift toward premium products in North America, which pressured single-class low-cost models ([IATA, 9 Dec 2025](https://www.iata.org/en/pressroom/2025-releases/2025-12-09-01/)).

**Leisure and price sensitivity.** IATA attributed strong European low-cost performance to intra-European traffic and leisure demand ([IATA, 9 Dec 2025](https://www.iata.org/en/pressroom/2025-releases/2025-12-09-01/)). On the downside, IATA linked mid-2026 domestic softness in large markets to higher jet-fuel prices feeding into fares, indicating meaningful price sensitivity in leisure-heavy domestic segments ([IATA, 30 Jul 2026](https://www.iata.org/en/pressroom/2026-releases/07-30-air-passenger-demand-falls-june/); corroborated by [Travel Trade Journal, 31 Jul 2026](https://traveltradejournal.com/global-air-passenger-demand-falls-1-7-per-cent-in-june-as-domestic-markets-weigh-on-growth-iata/)). Consumer sentiment nonetheless stayed high in IATA's October-November 2025 poll (14 countries, 6,500 respondents): **97%** were satisfied with their last trip, **88%** agreed air travel makes their lives better, and **78%** viewed it as good value ([IATA, 9 Dec 2025](https://www.iata.org/en/pressroom/2025-releases/2025-12-09-01/)).

**Behavioral note.** IBA observed that "late-booking behaviour" would be one factor flowing more fully through airline profit-and-loss in the second and third quarters, indicating shorter booking windows as a watch-item for 2026 revenue management ([IBA, 22 May 2026](https://www.iba.aero/resources/articles/2026-q1-airline-results/)).

## 8. Selective 2027-2030 Implications

Long-run structural demand remains firm despite the 2026 cost surge. Airbus's 2025 Global Market Forecast projects passenger traffic growing **3.6% annually** over 2024-2044, underpinned by GDP growth (~2.5% CAGR) and a middle class expanding by about **1.5 billion** people; it anticipates a global fleet roughly doubling from **24,730 (end-2024) to about 49,210 (2044)** and demand for about **43,420 new passenger and freighter aircraft** ([Airbus GMF 2025](https://www.airbus.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-airbus-global-market-forecast-2025-people-and-commerce-driving-air-traffic-growth)).

> "Despite short term uncertainties, in the long-term, passenger traffic will grow by 3.6% annually, driven by global GDP (+2.5%), urban populations (+1.2 billion) and an increase of 1.5 billion in the global middle class."
> - Source: [Airbus Global Market Forecast 2025 (11 June 2025)](https://www.airbus.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-airbus-global-market-forecast-2025-people-and-commerce-driving-air-traffic-growth)

Directional implications for 2027-2030 supported by the evidence:

- **Emerging-market and intra-regional flows lead growth.** Airbus identifies domestic India (**+8.9%**) and emerging-Asia-to-China (**+8.5%**) as the fastest-growing flows, versus mature-market flows nearer **3.8%** or below — consistent with IATA's near-term higher RPK growth in Asia Pacific, Latin America, and Africa ([Airbus GMF 2025 summary](https://www.airbus.com/sites/g/files/jlcbta136/files/2025-06/Summary%20GMF%202025-44.pdf)).
- **Capacity discipline supports load factors and yields.** IATA links record load factors partly to constrained aircraft supply, with the delivery backlog expected to keep rising and average fleet age at record highs — conditions that can persist beyond 2026 and sustain high utilization ([IATA, 9 Dec 2025](https://www.iata.org/en/pressroom/2025-releases/2025-12-09-01/)).
- **Structural affordability trend continues.** Both IATA (falling real fares) and Airbus (improving affordability via unit-cost reduction) point to a long-run downward real-fare trajectory once the 2026 fuel-cost pulse normalizes ([IATA, 9 Dec 2025](https://www.iata.org/en/pressroom/2025-releases/2025-12-09-01/); [Airbus GMF 2025](https://www.airbus.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-airbus-global-market-forecast-2025-people-and-commerce-driving-air-traffic-growth)).

## 9. Uncertainty and Divergent Evidence

- **Large intra-year forecast revision.** The most important uncertainty is the December-2025-to-June-2026 swing in the 2026 profit forecast ($41 B → $23 B). The two figures are not a data contradiction but a genuine reassessment as input costs changed; both are retained and labeled by vintage rather than averaged ([IATA, 9 Dec 2025](https://www.iata.org/en/pressroom/2025-releases/2025-12-09-01/); [IATA Global Outlook, Jun 2026](https://www.iata.org/en/publications/economics/reports/global-outlook-for-air-transport-june-2026/)).
- **Moving 2025 baseline.** The 2025 estimate itself rose across vintages ($36.6 B in Dec 2024 → $39.5 B in Dec 2025 → $45 B in Jun 2026), and 2025 revenue was revised from ~$1.008 T to ~$1.065 T. Year-over-year 2026 comparisons therefore depend on which 2025 baseline is used ([IATA, 10 Dec 2024](https://www.iata.org/en/pressroom/2024-releases/2024-12-10-01/); [IATA, 9 Dec 2025](https://www.iata.org/en/pressroom/2025-releases/2025-12-09-01/); [IATA Global Outlook, Jun 2026](https://www.iata.org/en/publications/economics/reports/global-outlook-for-air-transport-june-2026/)).
- **Revenue up while profit down.** The June revision raised 2026 revenue but halved profit, underscoring that the 2026 problem is a cost/margin problem, not a demand-collapse problem — a nuance that a single headline number can obscure ([IATA Global Outlook, Jun 2026](https://www.iata.org/en/publications/economics/reports/global-outlook-for-air-transport-june-2026/)).
- **Realized vs forecast demand.** IATA's full-year 2026 passenger forecast (~5.1 B, +2.4%) coexists with realized mid-year monthly contraction; the reconciliation assumes a stronger second half as comparisons ease. This is a forecast assumption, not a realized outcome ([IATA Global Outlook, Jun 2026](https://www.iata.org/en/publications/economics/reports/global-outlook-for-air-transport-june-2026/); [IATA APMA June 2026](https://www.iata.org/en/iata-repository/publications/economic-reports/air-passenger-market-analysis-june-2026/)).
- **Sample and source caveats.** IBA's Q1 2026 margins reflect early reporters (especially thin for Asia Pacific) and individual disclosures, not a full industry aggregate; GBTA business-travel figures are a single association's index (authoritative but not independently corroborated here); Airbus's long-run forecast is a manufacturer view that other forecasters may frame differently ([IBA, 22 May 2026](https://www.iba.aero/resources/articles/2026-q1-airline-results/); [CAPA / GBTA, Aug 2026](https://www.routesonline.com/suppliers/10554/capa-centre-for-aviation/news/299666765/business-travel-hits-record-spending-but-airlines-face-a-harder-growth-equation/); [Airbus GMF 2025](https://www.airbus.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-airbus-global-market-forecast-2025-people-and-commerce-driving-air-traffic-growth)).
- **Out-of-scope drivers.** Some source narratives attribute the mid-2026 change to factors outside this report's scope; only the neutral market and cost evidence is restated here, and regions are named solely as market or operating evidence.

## 10. Presentation-Ready Neutral Insights

Concise, factual synthesis for later slide development (not slide copy; not prescriptive):

1. **Two 2026 numbers, one year.** The industry's 2026 profit outlook moved from a record **$41 B / 3.9%** (Dec 2025) to **$23 B / 2.0%** (Jun 2026) — a ~50% cut driven mainly by a sharp (~70%) jet-fuel price rise and higher input costs.
2. **Demand reversed within the year.** RPK went from **+3.8% (Jan)** to **-2.2% (May)** and **-1.7% (Jun)** year-on-year — the first decline in over two years — yet load factors held near **84%**.
3. **Weakness was concentrated.** Excluding one region, June global demand fell only **0.6%**; most international markets kept growing.
4. **Revenue rose while profit fell.** 2026 revenue was revised **up** to **$1.165 T (+9.4%)** even as profit halved — a cost/margin story, not a demand-collapse story.
5. **Margins remain below the cost of capital.** ROIC of **4.3%** sits under an **8.5%** WACC in the June view; profit per passenger is about **$4.50**, versus $7.90-$9.10 in 2025 estimates.
6. **Dispersion is the story under the average.** European full-service EBIT margins improved (**-4.0% → -0.7%**) while European low-cost losses deepened; North American revenue grew ~**10%** on ~**2%** capacity.
7. **Demand is value-dense.** Business-travel spend is set for a record **$1.71 T (+7.2%)** on only **+1.3%** more trips; ~**42%** of business travelers fly premium.
8. **Ancillaries are structurally larger.** For the first time since 2019, ancillary revenue is projected to exceed cargo as a revenue contributor.
9. **Records amid constraint.** Load factor (**~84%**) and passenger volume (**~5.1 B**) remain at or near record highs, aided by constrained aircraft supply.
10. **Long-run demand intact.** A **3.6%** annual traffic-growth path toward 10 billion-plus passengers frames 2026 as a cost-driven margin pause rather than a structural demand break.

## 11. Research Limitations

- **Neutrality trade-off.** Several load-bearing 2026 sources attribute outcomes to excluded topics. This report restates only neutral cost, operating, and market evidence and names regions solely as market/operating evidence, which necessarily omits causal narrative present in the originals. For the June 2026 figures, body citations use the neutral IATA Global Outlook (June 2026) economic report; the itemized companion release is isolated in the reference list for figure-level traceability and is not cited in the body.
- **PDF extraction.** The IATA June 2026 Air Passenger Market Analysis returned as non-parseable PDF binary via direct fetch; its figures were captured from IATA's own summary text plus two independent secondary reports (AirGuide, Travel Trade Journal) that reproduce the same IATA data, providing corroboration of the June numbers.
- **Filtered/unreachable sources.** A McKinsey premium-cabin analysis was dropped by the browse provider and a direct fetch timed out, so premium-cabin structural detail relies on IATA and GBTA/CAPA instead. The IBA article was unavailable via fetch (HTTP 520) but was successfully retrieved via `WebIQ-MCP/browse`.
- **Single-source items.** GBTA business-travel spend ($1.71 T), the 42% premium-cabin share, Airbus's 3.6% long-run CAGR, and IBA's Q1 EBIT-margin ranges are each effectively single-source (though from authoritative originators) and are labeled accordingly rather than blended with other estimates.
- **Evidence gaps.** Visiting-friends-and-relatives (VFR) demand, granular booking-window data, and fare elasticity by segment are only partially covered by available 2026 sources; booking-window evidence is qualitative (IBA's "late-booking behaviour"). Full-year 2026 realized results were not yet available at the access date, so second-half 2026 remains forecast.
- **Currency and comparability.** Carrier disclosures mix USD and other currencies (for example, Air Canada in CAD); regional aggregates and carrier figures use different scopes and are not directly additive.
