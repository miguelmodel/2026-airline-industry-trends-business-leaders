## Scope, exclusions, and neutrality note

This report covers global airline jet-fuel price conditions and 2026 outlooks; physical
supply, refining, and generic disruption risk; airline fuel-cost exposure and hedging
prevalence as neutral evidence; and Sustainable Aviation Fuel (SAF) availability,
capacity, mandates, prices, book-and-claim, and feedstock/technology constraints, plus
the emissions-cost mechanisms that directly affect fuel economics.

By design, this report **excludes all external cause-attribution — political actors, named
international events, and market-restriction measures.** The 2026 fuel environment was shaped by a large
physical supply disruption that began in late February 2026. Consistent with scope, that
disruption is described **only in energy-market and operating terms** (reduced crude and
refined-product flows, route/logistics constraints, price and volatility effects), with
**no cause, actor, or event attribution.** The report also **prescribes no actions** and
uses neutral, evidence-led constructions throughout. Some retrieved primary sources framed
2026 around excluded topics; only their energy-market data has been retained here.

## 1. Executive summary

Airline fuel economics dominate the 2026 industry picture. After two years of easing
prices, a large, **supply-driven** shock in 2026 lifted crude and jet-fuel prices sharply
and widened refining margins. The International Air Transport Association (IATA) forecasts
an average jet-fuel price of **USD 152 per barrel in 2026, almost 70% above 2025**, pushing
the industry fuel bill from **USD 252 billion (2025) to USD 351 billion (2026)** and raising
fuel to **31.4% of operating costs** (from 25.4%). Fuel volumes are essentially flat
year-over-year; **price alone** drives the increase ([IATA Fuel Fact Sheet, Jun 2026](https://www.iata.org/en/iata-repository/pressroom/fact-sheets/fact-sheet-fuel/); [IATA Economics, 19 Jun 2026](https://www.iata.org/en/iata-repository/publications/economic-reports/profits-and-margins-to-shrink-in-2026-but-remain-positive/)).

Forecasts diverge and are unusually sensitive to whether the disruption persists. The U.S.
Energy Information Administration (EIA) — which in **February 2026** had projected Brent
*falling* to ~USD 58/b in 2026 — raised its **August 2026** forecast to **USD 87/b for 2026**
and **USD 69/b for 2027**, and set U.S. wholesale jet fuel at **USD 3.24/gal (2026)** easing
to **USD 2.50/gal (2027)** ([EIA STEO, 11 Aug 2026](https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/steo/); [EIA, 11 Feb 2026](https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=67164)). The record-wide jet **crack spread (~USD 57/b)** means crude-only hedges leave a large exposure uncovered.

SAF remains structurally scarce and expensive. Global SAF production is estimated at
**~2.4 million tonnes (Mt) in 2026 — about 0.8% of jet-fuel use — at a ~USD 4.3 billion
premium** ([IATA, 6 Jun 2026](https://www.iata.org/en/pressroom/2026-releases/06-06-saf-production-volumes-still-disappointing/)).
Operational capacity reached **5.9 Mt by mid-2026**, but independent research finds capacity
must grow **≥41% per year** to give production a realistic chance of meeting the ICAO CAAF/3
2030 vision ([Martulli & Malina, Jul 2026](https://zenodo-rdm.web.cern.ch/records/21230501/files/Update_mid2026_SAFcapacity_global_final.pdf)).
Electro-SAF (e-SAF) is the acute bottleneck: EU/UK mandates imply ~0.6 Mt by 2030 against
~0.02 Mt of operating-plus-under-construction capacity. Mandates (EU ReFuelEU: 2% in 2025 →
6% in 2030 → 70% in 2050) and the full removal of EU ETS free allowances for aviation from
1 January 2026 (carbon at €70–100/tonne) are now direct, rising line items in fuel economics.

## 3. Overview and key concepts

- **Jet fuel (Jet A / Jet A-1, kerosene-type):** a middle distillate refined from crude oil.
  Its price tracks crude (Brent is the common global benchmark) **plus** a refining margin.
- **Crack spread (jet):** the price difference between jet fuel and crude, in USD/bbl. It
  captures refining scarcity and is separate from crude price risk — a distinction that
  matters because most airline hedges reference crude, not jet ([IATA Fuel Fact Sheet, Jun 2026](https://www.iata.org/en/iata-repository/pressroom/fact-sheets/fact-sheet-fuel/)).
- **Fuel-cost exposure:** fuel is the single largest variable cost for most carriers,
  historically ~15–25% of operating expenses and ~31% in 2026 ([IATA Economics, 19 Jun 2026](https://www.iata.org/en/iata-repository/publications/economic-reports/profits-and-margins-to-shrink-in-2026-but-remain-positive/); [DWU Consulting, Aug 2026](https://dwuconsulting.com/dwu-ai/airline-fuel-hedging-strategies)).
- **Fuel hedging:** the use of derivatives (swaps/options, often on crude or refined jet)
  to fix a share of future fuel cost. Presented here strictly as neutral industry evidence.
- **SAF (Sustainable Aviation Fuel):** a drop-in kerosene substitute certified under
  ASTM D7566, blendable up to 50% with fossil jet. Main pathways: **HEFA** (from used cooking
  oil, tallow, vegetable oils; ~85% of 2025 volume), **AtJ** (alcohol-to-jet), and **PtL/e-SAF**
  (power-to-liquid from green hydrogen + CO₂). Lifecycle emissions savings run ~65–100%
  depending on pathway ([EcoFreight, May 2026](https://ecofreight.co/blog/saf-supply-math); [EUR-Lex, ReFuelEU summary](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=legissum%3A4714799)).
- **Production vs capacity:** *announced capacity* (project pipeline) ≫ *operational capacity*
  (built plants) ≫ *actual production volume* (fuel produced in-year). Conflating them
  overstates supply ([Martulli & Malina, Jul 2026](https://zenodo-rdm.web.cern.ch/records/21230501/files/Update_mid2026_SAFcapacity_global_final.pdf)).
- **Book-and-claim:** a chain-of-custody accounting system that lets an airline buy SAF's
  environmental attributes even when physical SAF is uplifted elsewhere — used to overcome
  SAF's geographic scarcity ([IATA SAF Registry, 3 Apr 2025](https://www.iata.org/en/pressroom/2025-releases/2025-04-03-01/)).
- **Emissions-cost mechanisms:** the **EU ETS** (cap-and-trade; airlines surrender
  allowances for CO₂ on covered routes) and **CORSIA** (ICAO's global offsetting scheme).
  Both convert emissions into a cash cost that interacts with fuel economics ([Norton Rose Fulbright, Aug 2026](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/043fcad6/the-eus-latest-proposals-for-the-eu-ets-its-interaction-with-corsia); [Aviation Reg Watch, Jun 2026](https://aviationregwatch.com/eu-ets-aviation-free-allowances/)).

## 4. Key findings

1. **A supply-driven 2026 price shock, not a demand story.** IATA reports fuel consumption
   is flat year-over-year in 2026; the entire fuel-cost increase is price-driven. IATA's
   2026 average jet-fuel forecast is **USD 152/b (~+70% vs ~USD 90/b in 2025)** and the jet
   crack spread is expected to average a record **~USD 57/b** ([IATA Fuel Fact Sheet, Jun 2026](https://www.iata.org/en/iata-repository/pressroom/fact-sheets/fact-sheet-fuel/)).

   > "we forecast an average jet fuel price of USD 152 per barrel in 2026—almost 70% higher than in 2025."
   > - Source: [IATA Fuel Fact Sheet, June 2026](https://www.iata.org/en/iata-repository/pressroom/fact-sheets/fact-sheet-fuel/)

2. **Forecasts swung hard and remain conditional.** EIA's **February 2026** outlook expected
   Brent to *fall* to ~USD 58/b in 2026; its **August 2026** outlook raised 2026 to **USD 87/b**
   and set 2027 at **USD 69/b**, assuming the disruption eases and production recovers in early
   2027 ([EIA, 11 Feb 2026](https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=67164); [EIA STEO, 11 Aug 2026](https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/steo/)). The ~USD 29/b upward revision in six months illustrates the volatility.

3. **The industry fuel bill jumped ~USD 100 billion.** IATA's June 2026 outlook halved
   projected 2026 net profit to **USD 23 billion** and put the fuel bill at **USD 351 billion**,
   or **31.4% of operating costs** ([IATA Economics, 19 Jun 2026](https://www.iata.org/en/iata-repository/publications/economic-reports/profits-and-margins-to-shrink-in-2026-but-remain-positive/)).

   > "The industry's fuel bill is set to reach USD 351 billion, lifting fuel's share of total operating costs to 31.4%."
   > - Source: [IATA Economics, "Profits and Margins to Shrink in 2026," 19 June 2026](https://www.iata.org/en/iata-repository/publications/economic-reports/profits-and-margins-to-shrink-in-2026-but-remain-positive/)

4. **Physical supply and refining were structurally tight before the shock.** In February
   2025, EIA forecast U.S. jet-fuel days-of-supply falling to **~21 days in 2026 — the lowest
   since 1963** — with U.S. jet consumption at an all-time high and the three main transport
   fuels' inventories ending 2026 near **375 million barrels (lowest since 2000)**, driven by
   two refinery closures ([EIA, 3 Mar 2025](https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=64644)). In 2026, global refinery crude runs were expected to **fall ~1 mb/d to ~82.9 mb/d**, with middle-distillate cracks hitting record highs ([IEA Oil Market Report, Apr 2026](https://www.iea.org/reports/oil-market-report-april-2026)).

   > "we forecast U.S. jet fuel will decline to about 21 days of supply—the lowest since 1963."
   > - Source: [EIA, "Refinery closures and rising consumption will reduce U.S. petroleum inventories in 2026," 3 March 2025](https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=64644)

5. **Hedging prevalence is uneven and crude-centric.** Globally, airlines hedged **~one-third
   of 2026 fuel**; European carriers entered 2026 near **70%**, while North American majors
   hold **minimal or zero** financial hedges (global/regional averages are IATA Global Outlook
   figures as summarized by [The Frequent Flier, Jun 2026](https://thefrequentflier.com/briefing/iata-2026-outlook-fuel-sorting) — a secondary summary of IATA; carrier-level positions from [Reuters via RTÉ, 3 Mar 2026](https://www.rte.ie/news/business/2026/0303/1561435-airlines-hedge-against-fuel-price-hikes/), compiling company disclosures). Because many hedges reference **crude, not jet**, the record crack spread is largely uncovered ([IATA Fuel Fact Sheet, Jun 2026](https://www.iata.org/en/iata-repository/pressroom/fact-sheets/fact-sheet-fuel/)).

6. **SAF supply is scaling but remains <1% of fuel.** 2026 SAF production is estimated at
   **~2.4 Mt (≈0.8% of jet-fuel use)** after **1.0 Mt (2024)** and **1.9 Mt (2025)** ([IATA, 6 Jun 2026](https://www.iata.org/en/pressroom/2026-releases/06-06-saf-production-volumes-still-disappointing/); [Martulli & Malina, Jul 2026](https://zenodo-rdm.web.cern.ch/records/21230501/files/Update_mid2026_SAFcapacity_global_final.pdf); [BloombergNEF via OFI Magazine, 7 Aug 2026](https://www.ofimagazine.com/news/global-saf-prices-surge-by-10)).

   > "global Sustainable Aviation Fuel (SAF) production is expected to reach around 2.4 million tonnes in 2026, representing just 0.8% of aviation fuel use, at a cost to airlines of $4.3 billion."
   > - Source: [IATA, "SAF production volumes still disappointing," 6 June 2026](https://www.iata.org/en/pressroom/2026-releases/06-06-saf-production-volumes-still-disappointing/)

7. **e-SAF is the binding bottleneck.** EU and UK mandates imply **~0.6 Mt of e-SAF by 2030**
   versus **~0.02 Mt** of operating-plus-under-construction capacity, with no new final
   investment decisions (FIDs) in the past year ([IATA, 6 Jun 2026](https://www.iata.org/en/pressroom/2026-releases/06-06-saf-production-volumes-still-disappointing/)). Independent tracking finds only **2–3% of announced e-SAF capacity has reached FID** ([Martulli & Malina, Jul 2026](https://zenodo-rdm.web.cern.ch/records/21230501/files/Update_mid2026_SAFcapacity_global_final.pdf)).

8. **SAF prices are a multiple of fossil jet and rising.** BloombergNEF's 2026 survey put
   HEFA SAF at **USD 1,817/tonne** (≈USD 5.54/gal), advanced bio-SAF at **~USD 3,399/tonne**,
   and e-SAF at **~USD 5,015/tonne (~7× conventional)**, with SAF up **~10% year-over-year**
   ([BloombergNEF via OFI Magazine, 7 Aug 2026](https://www.ofimagazine.com/news/global-saf-prices-surge-by-10)). IATA uses a blended SAF assumption of **USD 2,872/tonne (USD 374/b)** for its 2026 premium of **USD 4.3 billion** ([IATA Fuel Fact Sheet, Jun 2026](https://www.iata.org/en/iata-repository/pressroom/fact-sheets/fact-sheet-fuel/)).

9. **Emissions costs now bite directly.** EU ETS free allowances for aviation phase out
   **25% in 2024, 50% in 2025, and 100% from 2026** — i.e., allowances are fully auctioned from
   1 January 2026 ([EUR-Lex, Directive (EU) 2023/958](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2023/958); [European Parliament final act, 16 May 2023](https://oeil.europarl.europa.eu/oeil/en/procedure-document-summary/pdf?id=1745247)). Secondary market reporting puts the 2026 carbon price at an estimated **€70–100/tonne**, with ~330 Mt of allowances to be surrendered across 2026–2030 ([Aviation Reg Watch, Jun 2026](https://aviationregwatch.com/eu-ets-aviation-free-allowances/) — secondary). A July 2026 EU proposal would extend ETS scope to flights within 5,000 km of the EU from **2029**, formalize CORSIA into EU law, and let carriers deduct CORSIA costs to avoid double-charging ([Norton Rose Fulbright, Aug 2026](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/043fcad6/the-eus-latest-proposals-for-the-eu-ets-its-interaction-with-corsia)).

available at scale ([EcoFreight, May 2026](https://ecofreight.co/blog/saf-supply-math); [Energy-Solutions.co, Aug 2026](https://energy-solutions.co/articles/sub/sustainable-aviation-fuel-hefa-vs-alcohol-to-jet-economics)).

## 6. Regional variation

Fuel economics diverge by region on energy-market and operating grounds, independent of any
external narrative:

- **Hedge coverage.** European carriers entered 2026 with the highest coverage (~70%),
  cushioning near-term cost pass-through; the global average is ~33%; North American majors
  carry minimal financial hedges, so cost changes reach their base more directly ([The Frequent Flier, Jun 2026](https://thefrequentflier.com/briefing/iata-2026-outlook-fuel-sorting), a secondary summary of IATA's June 2026 Global Outlook; carrier-level disclosures via [Reuters/RTÉ, 3 Mar 2026](https://www.rte.ie/news/business/2026/0303/1561435-airlines-hedge-against-fuel-price-hikes/)).
- **Refining and import dependence.** Regions that import crude or refined jet fuel — and
  those with weaker local currencies against the U.S. dollar (in which jet fuel is priced) —
  faced higher effective fuel costs in 2026; European and Asian middle-distillate markets
  tightened notably, with Asian middle-distillate prices reaching record highs ([IEA OMR, Apr 2026](https://www.iea.org/reports/oil-market-report-april-2026); [The Frequent Flier, Jun 2026](https://thefrequentflier.com/briefing/iata-2026-outlook-fuel-sorting), secondary summary of IATA).
- **Generic operating constraints.** Some regions absorbed added fuel burn from route and
  airspace constraints in 2026; these are noted only as operating effects, without cause
  attribution ([The Frequent Flier, Jun 2026](https://thefrequentflier.com/briefing/iata-2026-outlook-fuel-sorting), a secondary summary of IATA).
- **SAF and mandate exposure.** SAF obligations concentrate cost in **mandated markets**
  (EU 2% in 2025 rising to 6% in 2030; UK 2% in 2025 rising to 10% in 2030, with a £4.70/litre
  buy-out ceiling), and NW-Europe HEFA prices (peaking ~USD 2,830/tonne) ran above the global
  average, so European operators face the earliest and steepest blended-fuel premiums
  ([EUR-Lex ReFuelEU](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=legissum%3A4714799); [GOV.UK — SAF Mandate](https://www.gov.uk/government/collections/sustainable-aviation-fuel-saf-mandate); [BloombergNEF via OFI, Aug 2026](https://www.ofimagazine.com/news/global-saf-prices-surge-by-10); [Energy-Solutions.co, Aug 2026](https://energy-solutions.co/articles/sub/sustainable-aviation-fuel-hefa-vs-alcohol-to-jet-economics)).
- **Carbon-cost geography.** EU ETS costs fall on intra-EEA flying now; a proposed 2029
  extension to flights within 5,000 km of the EU would broaden exposure for some
  medium-haul routes ([Norton Rose Fulbright, Aug 2026](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/043fcad6/the-eus-latest-proposals-for-the-eu-ets-its-interaction-with-corsia)).

## 7. Uncertainty and evidence disagreements

- **2026 price level (methodology gap).** IATA's global average jet-fuel forecast (USD 152/b)
  sits above EIA's implied U.S. wholesale level (~USD 136/b from USD 3.24/gal). The gap
  reflects global-vs-U.S. scope, wholesale-vs-delivered basis, and IATA's assumption that the
  disruption persists. Both agree directionally on a ~50–70% year-over-year rise ([IATA, Jun 2026](https://www.iata.org/en/iata-repository/pressroom/fact-sheets/fact-sheet-fuel/); [EIA, Aug 2026](https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/steo/tables/pdf/2tab.pdf)).
- **2027 normalization is conditional.** EIA's USD 69/b Brent for 2027 assumes production
  recovers in early 2027; a persistent disruption would keep prices and crack spreads elevated.
  The six-month swing from EIA's USD 58/b (Feb) to USD 87/b (Aug) 2026 forecast shows how fast
  the base case can move ([EIA, 11 Feb 2026](https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=67164); [EIA STEO, Aug 2026](https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/steo/)).
- **SAF's share of fuel (denominator sensitivity).** Reported 2025 SAF shares range from
  ~0.3% (using a ~400 Mt global jet-fuel denominator) to ~0.6% (BloombergNEF) — while the
  *production volumes* (1.9 Mt in 2025; ~2.4 Mt in 2026) are consistent across IATA,
  BloombergNEF, and academic tracking. Percentages should be read with their denominator
  ([EcoFreight, May 2026](https://ecofreight.co/blog/saf-supply-math); [BloombergNEF via OFI, Aug 2026](https://www.ofimagazine.com/news/global-saf-prices-surge-by-10); [IATA, Jun 2026](https://www.iata.org/en/pressroom/2026-releases/06-06-saf-production-volumes-still-disappointing/)).
- **SAF "multiple" over fossil jet is unstable.** BloombergNEF frames SAF's premium as
  "around five times" fossil jet, yet the reported absolute prices (HEFA ~USD 1,817/t vs
  conventional ~USD 1,190/t in Aug 2026) imply ~1.5× — because 2026's fossil-jet spike
  compressed the multiple even as the absolute premium stayed large. The multiple is highly
  sensitive to the conventional-price baseline used ([BloombergNEF via OFI, Aug 2026](https://www.ofimagazine.com/news/global-saf-prices-surge-by-10); [Energy-Solutions.co, Aug 2026](https://energy-solutions.co/articles/sub/sustainable-aviation-fuel-hefa-vs-alcohol-to-jet-economics)).
- **Announced vs realized SAF capacity.** Project pipelines imply large 2030 supply, but only
  12% of 2030-planned capacity is operational and 26% of 2026-planned capacity is paused or
  cancelled — so pipeline totals overstate deliverable volume ([Martulli & Malina, Jul 2026](https://zenodo-rdm.web.cern.ch/records/21230501/files/Update_mid2026_SAFcapacity_global_final.pdf)).
- **Source-quality caveats.** EU ETS price/allowance figures and several SAF price/UCO points
  come from trade or consulting analyses (Aviation Reg Watch, DWU Consulting, Energy-Solutions.co,
  EcoFreight, The Frequent Flier). They are corroborated where possible against IATA, EUR-Lex,
  BloombergNEF, and Norton Rose Fulbright, and are flagged as secondary. IATA's June 2026
  profit/fuel-bill figures are the primary anchor for the industry totals.

## 8. Presentation-ready neutral insights

The following are framed as evidence, not guidance, for the leadership discussion:

- **Fuel returned as the swing factor in 2026.** The data show fuel rising to ~31% of
  operating costs and adding ~USD 100 billion to the industry bill — driven by price, with
  volumes flat ([IATA, 19 Jun 2026](https://www.iata.org/en/iata-repository/publications/economic-reports/profits-and-margins-to-shrink-in-2026-but-remain-positive/)).
- **The 2026 shock was supply-side and forecast-volatile.** EIA's 2026 Brent view moved from
  ~USD 58/b to ~USD 87/b within six months; 2027 outlooks (~USD 69/b) imply normalization if
  supply recovers ([EIA, Feb 2026](https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=67164); [EIA STEO, Aug 2026](https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/steo/)).
- **Crude hedges leave the refining margin exposed.** With the jet crack spread at a record
  ~USD 57/b and most hedges referencing crude, hedge coverage protects less of the 2026 bill
  than the headline ratios suggest ([IATA, Jun 2026](https://www.iata.org/en/iata-repository/pressroom/fact-sheets/fact-sheet-fuel/); [The Frequent Flier, Jun 2026](https://thefrequentflier.com/briefing/iata-2026-outlook-fuel-sorting)).
- **SAF remains <1% of fuel and multiples above fossil jet.** The evidence indicates a
  persistent supply-demand gap and an e-SAF investment bottleneck through 2030 ([IATA, Jun 2026](https://www.iata.org/en/pressroom/2026-releases/06-06-saf-production-volumes-still-disappointing/); [Martulli & Malina, Jul 2026](https://zenodo-rdm.web.cern.ch/records/21230501/files/Update_mid2026_SAFcapacity_global_final.pdf)).
- **Policy is turning fuel decarbonization into a hard cost.** Rising EU/UK blending mandates,
  full EU ETS carbon pricing from 2026, and CORSIA Phase 2 from 2027 convert emissions and
  fuel choices into direct, escalating expenses ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=legissum%3A4714799); [Aviation Reg Watch, Jun 2026](https://aviationregwatch.com/eu-ets-aviation-free-allowances/); [Norton Rose Fulbright, Aug 2026](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/043fcad6/the-eus-latest-proposals-for-the-eu-ets-its-interaction-with-corsia)).
- **Book-and-claim is emerging market infrastructure.** IATA's SAF Registry (live since
  April 2025, run by CADO) lets airlines claim SAF attributes regardless of uplift location,
  a structural response to SAF's geographic scarcity ([IATA, 3 Apr 2025](https://www.iata.org/en/pressroom/2025-releases/2025-04-03-01/)).

> "It ensures that all airlines in the world have access to SAF and that their SAF purchases can be claimed against any climate-related obligations in this domain."
> - Source: [IATA, "SAF Registry Goes Live," 3 April 2025](https://www.iata.org/en/pressroom/2025-releases/2025-04-03-01/)

## 9. Research limitations

- **Event-saturated primary sources.** Most 2026 fuel sources framed the year around excluded
  topics. Only their energy-market data was retained, and the disruption is described purely
  as a supply/logistics constraint. Readers seeking cause analysis will not find it here by
  design.
- **U.S.-centric high-frequency data.** The cleanest supply figures (days of supply, refinery
  runs, wholesale jet) are U.S. or global-aggregate; comparable audited per-region jet-fuel
  price series were not uniformly available in open sources.
- **2025 U.S. wholesale jet-fuel figure is approximate**, read from EIA's quarterly series;
  an all-in U.S. 2025 figure (~USD 2.40/gal) is from a secondary compilation of BTS data.
- **SAF price coverage.** BloombergNEF's survey (actual contract prices) is a strong anchor
  but was accessed via trade-press reporting rather than the primary subscription report;
  regional SAF spot peaks come from a secondary analysis.
- **EU ETS, CORSIA, and UK claims are now anchored to primary law.** The EU free-allowance
  phase-out (25%/50%/100%), current scope, and SAF price-differential support (95%/70%/50%) are
  sourced to Directive (EU) 2023/958 and the European Parliament; CORSIA phase timing to ICAO;
  and the UK SAF Mandate trajectory and £4.70/£5.00-per-litre buy-outs to GOV.UK/DfT and SI
  2024/1187. The **2026 EU ETS carbon price (est. €70–100/t) and ~330 Mt allowance total** remain
  secondary/market estimates that fluctuate; secondary trade analyses are retained only as
  corroboration or explicit context.
- **Proposed EU ETS scope changes (2029 extension, SAF support extended to 2040)** are draft
  legislation as of the reporting date and may change before adoption.
- **IEA Oil Market Report (April 2026 edition)** is issued as an abridged free summary (fuller
  data is subscription-gated); only its structural figures (crude runs ~82.9 mb/d, record
  middle-distillate cracks) were used, generically. The correct April 2026 edition is cited
  throughout, replacing an earlier misattributed January link.
- **No prescriptive content or hedging/procurement guidance** is included, consistent with
  scope; hedging data is descriptive only.
